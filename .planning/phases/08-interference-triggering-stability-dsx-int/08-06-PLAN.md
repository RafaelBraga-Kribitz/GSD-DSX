<corrections_applied>
Both amendments are corrections, and both must be recorded as corrections rather than slipped in.

**Success criterion 3 asks for something the paper does not contain.** It requires a test asserting
the dilution identity "against the Deng & Hu (2015) published value". Primary-text research read all
ten pages of the camera-ready. There is no additive worked example anywhere in it; every number in
the paper is for a ratio metric. Decision D-10 rewords the criterion to the paper's published
**counterexample** — its time-to-success case, a true effect of −26 msec against the naive formula's
−18 msec. That metric is a ratio, so the same test simultaneously proves the additive-only scope
boundary REQ-P8-04 demands. This is a factual correction, not a lowered bar: the reworded criterion
is testable and the original was not.

**Success criterion 4 and REQ-P8-04 defer on a premise that is false.** Both say ratio-metric
dilution stays out of scope until "the Deng & Hu (2015) ratio-metric equation is obtained from
primary source". The paper is freely and publicly available at
`https://alexdeng.github.io/public/files/wsdm2015-dilution.pdf`, and its ratio equation — Formula (3)
in section 3.3 — is readable now. The condition as written is therefore already met and would unblock
the item immediately, which is not what anyone intended. The real blocker is mathematical: Formula (3)
sums over individual users and has no closed-form scalar multiplier, so it requires per-user data a
declaration-only gate never has. Decision D-12 rewrites the condition to name that, and notes the item
may be permanently out of scope rather than merely deferred.
</corrections_applied>
