    The cells, and the channel argument for each, which must appear as a short comment beside the
    cell it justifies. The structural criterion is: a mitigation is admissible only where it
    operates on the same interference channel the risk names.

    `none` maps to an empty set and the check short-circuits before consulting it.

    `shared_budget` — the channel is a shared, capacity-limited pool. Admissible:
    `budget_isolation`, because each arm drawing from a separate non-competing budget removes the
    pool itself; `time_split`, because arms separated in time never draw from the pool
    concurrently, so there is no competition to leak through; `modelled`, because estimating and
    adjusting for the interference addresses the same channel statistically rather than by design.
    Inadmissible and load-bearing: `cluster_randomisation`, because randomising at a coarser
    assignment grain changes who is assigned together but every cluster still draws from the one
    budget — the channel is the pool, not the assignment unit. Also inadmissible: `geo_split`, for
    the same reason at geography grain.

    `marketplace` — the channel is the matching or auction mechanism through which one side's
    treatment moves the other side's outcomes. Admissible: `cluster_randomisation`, because
    randomising whole market segments keeps both sides of an interaction inside one arm;
    `geo_split`, because a local market is the natural cluster when the market is geographic;
    `time_split`, because the two sides never meet across arms; `modelled`. Inadmissible:
    `budget_isolation`, because separate budgets do not stop the two market sides interacting.

    `geo_spillover` — the channel is geographic adjacency. Admissible: `geo_split`, directly;
    `cluster_randomisation`, because a geography is a cluster and clustering at or above the
    leakage scale contains it; `modelled`. Inadmissible: `time_split`, because separating in time
    does not stop spatial leakage and substitutes a temporal confound for it; `budget_isolation`,
    because the channel is not a budget.

    `social_graph` — the channel is the edges of a social or referral graph. Admissible:
    `cluster_randomisation`, because graph communities as clusters keep edges inside one arm;
    `modelled`. Inadmissible: `geo_split`, because social edges cross geographies; `time_split`,
    because a unit treated in one period still influences its neighbours in the next;
    `budget_isolation`, because the channel is not a budget.

    `shared_inventory` — the channel is a shared, finite stock. Admissible: `budget_isolation`,
    which is the vocabulary's only primitive for splitting a shared pool per arm and applies to an
    inventory pool as much as a budget; `time_split`, because arms never draw concurrently;
    `cluster_randomisation`, because clustering at the inventory-pool grain such as a warehouse or
    a regional stock pool contains the draw; `modelled`. Inadmissible: `geo_split`, because
    geography is not the inventory grain unless inventory happens to be regional, which
    `cluster_randomisation` already covers at the correct grain.

    Two facts from the verified publisher index must be stated in a comment and not papered over.
    `modelled` has no index entry in Kohavi, Tang and Xu's Chapter 22 at all — it is not a

    past `DSX-INT-011`. And the book carries a fifth technique the vocabulary omits, network
    egocentric randomization at page 233 — note it, do not add it, because a vocabulary member is
    contract surface this phase is not scoped for.
