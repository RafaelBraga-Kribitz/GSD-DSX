## Question 1 — `DSX-PAR-002` is HIGH, so does the undeclared-paradigm escape close at `plan`?

**Answer: `DSX-PAR-002` stays HIGH exactly as D-02 locks it, and the undeclared-paradigm escape is
nonetheless closed at `CRITICAL` at `dsx gate plan` — by the undeclared case selecting *both* halves
of the pair rather than by spending a new code number.**

The problem is real and currently open. `_validate_inference_shape` returns early when `inference:`
is absent or empty and skips blank values inside its membership loop, so omitting
`inference.paradigm` produces no finding at all today. The moment the pair ships, that becomes the
cheapest escape from both halves: `_PARADIGM_CONDITIONAL` selects nothing for a blank paradigm, so
neither code fires and an uncontrolled-continuous design clears `dsx gate plan` with exit `0`.
Deleting one line would be cheaper than declaring a paradigm honestly. That is the exact brief-D-10
distortion arriving through a door brief D-10 was not written to guard.

D-07 framed two options — accept the plan/verify asymmetry, or split requiredness onto its own
CRITICAL code. Neither is taken. A third resolution is better on every axis:

1. **It closes the escape at the gate point ROADMAP Success Criterion 1 depends on.** The pair is
   CRITICAL, and `GATE_THRESHOLDS` is CRITICAL at `plan` and `execute`, so an undeclared-paradigm
   uncontrolled-continuous design exits `1` at `dsx gate plan`.
2. **It spends no permanent code number.** Finding codes are never renumbered (brief D-06), so a new
   `DSX-PAR-0xx` for requiredness would be irreversible; this resolution coins nothing.
3. **It preserves brief D-10 exactly, not approximately.** D-10 forbids blocking *because* a
   paradigm is unsupported or undeclared. Under this design, declaring a paradigm never *adds* a
   finding — it only ever removes one, taking the possible findings from two to one. An honest
   declaration is therefore never more expensive than silence, which is the precise property D-10
   protects. What blocks is the undeclared *discipline*, not the undeclared *paradigm*.
4. **It is the structurally symmetric reading of the existing map.** "Undeclared" is not a third
   branch to be written by hand; it is "no branch has been excluded". Selecting the union falls out
   of the data-driven map rather than being a bespoke case.
5. **D-07's preferred mechanism survives intact.** `DSX-PAR-002` still carries the requiredness
   *message* for a missing `inference.paradigm`, at HIGH, so the whole escape story stays inside one
   family for the REQ-P9-06 audit to enumerate in one place. That half ships in plan 09-05.

**The asymmetry that is accepted, stated plainly:** `DSX-PAR-002`'s own finding does not block at
`plan`. That is correct rather than merely tolerable — a missing *justification* is not as severe as
an uncontrolled *design*, and the uncontrolled design is now blocked at `plan` regardless of whether
a paradigm was declared.

## Question 2 — does `DSX-PAR-011` perform the numeric `1/(K+1)` comparison on the gate path?

**Answer: presence only. No arithmetic is performed on any operator-declared value on the gate
path, in either half.**

1. **Symmetry decides it.** The frequentist half performs no arithmetic to decide whether to block —
   it calls `inflation_from_peeking()` to write the finding's detail text, not to make a decision.
   Giving the Bayesian half a numeric blocking comparison the frequentist half does not have would
   itself be the asymmetry brief D-12 forbids. This is the strongest of the three reasons.
2. **It removes the only injection surface this phase would otherwise open.** Extracting a
   probability from a free-text `decision_threshold` such as a posterior-probability statement
   requires a grammar over operator-authored prose. The research's own security section names that
   as the one tampering surface Phase 9 could introduce. Not parsing it removes the surface rather
   than hardening it.
3. **The comparison would prove nothing the gate can trust.** A declared calibration figure is the
   operator's own claim about their own calibration. Comparing a self-declared number against a
   self-derived bound is the project's standing known limit — a frame that lies passes — dressed up
   as arithmetic.

The number is asserted where it can be asserted honestly: on the `Reference value:` docstring line,
in a unit test over that literal, and in the seeded simulation from plan 09-02.
</resolved_open_questions>

**The noun that is now primary:** *the monitoring-discipline requirement, one per member of
`PARADIGMS`*. It is no longer "the frequentist peeking check, plus a Bayesian one added alongside".

**Decision: promote.** The general representation becomes primary and the specific ones are demoted
to rows of it. `_MONITORING_DISCIPLINE` — a dict keyed by every member of `PARADIGMS`, each row
naming that paradigm's finding code and its tuple of clearing declarations — is the primary object.
"The frequentist half" and "the Bayesian half" are two rows selected by one shared code path, not two
branches. This follows the promote decision `dsx/frame/paradigm.py` already made in Phase 6 for
`_PARADIGM_CONDITIONAL`, and it is what makes REQ-P9-05's both-directions property fall out of the
structure instead of being two hand-written assertions.

**Rationale:** adding a Bayesian branch alongside a still-required frequentist branch would silently
contradict the generalized intent — the whole point of brief D-12 is that neither paradigm is the
default one with the other bolted on.

**Contract test encoding the generalized intent** (required, not optional, because it is cheap and
it is the mechanical form of the symmetry claim): `set(_MONITORING_DISCIPLINE)` equals
`set(PARADIGMS)`; every row's clearing tuple has the same length; `threshold_calibration` appears in
every row; and each row has exactly one paradigm-specific clearing declaration. A future phase that
reintroduces a singular assumption, or adds a third paradigm without a row, goes red immediately.
</assumption_delta_decision>
