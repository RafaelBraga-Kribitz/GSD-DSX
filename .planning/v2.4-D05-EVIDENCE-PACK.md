## Tier 1 — code-critical

### T1-1 Cleveland & McGill 1984 — **CORRECTED, DO NOT SHIP AS ORIGINALLY WRITTEN**

**Source:** JASA 79(387):531–554, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1984.10478080. Primary text
read in full (24pp, OCR'd JSTOR scan via a university mirror).

**What the pack originally claimed:** a 7-item strict ordering — position-common >
position-nonaligned > length > angle/slope > area > volume > colour/saturation.

**What the paper actually publishes (p. 536, verbatim):**

> "The following are the 10 elementary tasks in Figure 1, ordered from most to
> least accurate: 1. Position along a common scale 2. Positions along nonaligned
> scales 3. Length, direction, angle 4. Area 5. Volume, curvature 6. Shading,
> color saturation"

**And the decisive caveat (p. 537, verbatim):**

> "Three of the ranks—3, 5, and 6—have more than one task; at the moment there is
> not enough information to separate the ties."

**Errors this corrects:**
- **length and angle are TIED at rank 3**, together with direction. The original
  list put length strictly above angle — the one relation the paper refuses.
- **"density" does not appear in the 1984 paper at all** (full-text grep: zero
  hits). It comes from Mackinlay (1986) via popularized versions.
- **"curvature" (rank 5, tied with volume) and "shading" (rank 6, tied with
  colour saturation) were silently dropped.**
- The paper calls the ordering **hypothesized** (§3 heading, p. 535); only
  position-vs-length and position-vs-angle were experimentally tested in 1984
  (p. 537). Area/volume/curvature/shading/colour are conjecture in this paper.
- Provenance of the original 7-item list traced to a Heer CS448B teaching slide
  and Mackinlay 1986 — the project's version matched **neither cleanly**.

**SHIPPING FORM (per operator decision D-1):**

```
rank 1: position_common_scale
rank 2: position_nonaligned_scales
rank 3: length, direction/slope, angle      # TIE — must not be separated
rank 4: area
rank 5: volume, curvature                   # TIE
rank 6: shading, colour_saturation          # TIE
```

Assertions must use `rank(a) <= rank(b)`. `rank(length) == rank(angle)` is
correct and citable; `rank(length) < rank(angle)` is **false** and must not ship.

**Status: SIGNED as corrected — cite p. 536 for the ordering, p. 537 for the ties.**

### T1-2 Heer & Bostock 2010 — **PARTIAL; "clean replication" was an overstatement**

**Source:** CHI '10 pp. 203–212, DOI 10.1145/1753326.1753357. Primary text read in
full (author-hosted PDF, idl.cs.washington.edu).

**Confirmed:** it does replicate Cleveland & McGill's proportional-judgment work —
but **only the position-vs-length portion** (judgment types T1–T5). Types 6 (angle)
and 7 (circular area) are *new* types Heer & Bostock designed, not replications.
Paper's own words (p. 205): *"The new results are similar (though not identical) to
the originals."*

**The code-critical finding (p. 206, verbatim):**

> "Theory also suggests that angle should perform worse than length, but the
> results do not support this. Cleveland & McGill also did not find angle to
> perform worse than length…"

So the `length > angle` link has **no support in either citation**. This
independently corroborates the T1-1 correction.

**Also noted:** the paper never tested volume, curvature, shading or colour
saturation — the bottom half of the ordering is untouched by it. Its rectangular-
area/treemap work (Exp 1B) is a genuine *extension*, including a counter-intuitive
result that 1:1 aspect ratios performed **worst**.

**Status: SIGNED as corrected — cite as a partial replication of the
position-vs-length ranking, never as confirmation of a full ordering.**

### T1-3 Wilke 2019 ch.16 — **CORRECTED; 2 of 4 proposed names do not exist**

**Source:** clauswilke.com/dataviz, read directly, plus the book's full-text
`search_index.json` covering all 34 pages (so absence claims are book-wide).

| Proposed id | Verdict | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| fan chart | **ABSENT from the entire book** | zero hits book-wide; nearest geometry is "graded confidence band" but Wilke never uses "fan chart" |
| quantile dot plot | **CONFIRMED** | "quantile dotplot", §16.1 Fig 16.3 (ch.5 spells it "Quantile Dot Plot") |
| half-eye | **name real, locator wrong** | ch.16 §16.2 only *mentions* "half eyes" and defers to ch.5; the illustrated form is §5.6 |
| gradient CI band | **NOT Wilke's term** | he has **two distinct marks**: "confidence strips" (continuous fade, Fig 16.9e) and "graded confidence band" (discrete nested levels, Fig 16.17) |

**Paradigm symmetry — CONFIRMED strongly** (this part of the claim holds).
§16.2 verbatim: *"Frequentists assess uncertainty with confidence intervals,
whereas Bayesians calculate posterior distributions and credible intervals."*
Fig 16.13 explicitly compares the two; Fig 16.14 shows Bayesian posteriors. The
D-12a-symmetric framing is genuinely supported.

**SHIPPING FORM (per operator decision D-2): adopt Wilke's own §5.6 vocabulary —
10 marks, each with a real locator:** error bars · graded error bars · 2D error
bars · confidence strips · eyes · half-eyes · quantile dot plot · confidence band ·
graded confidence band · fitted draws.

Note Wilke **distinguishes** "eye" (violin + error bar) from "half-eye" (ridgeline
+ error bar) — they are not interchangeable.

**Status: SIGNED as corrected.**

## Tier 3 — refusal-entry doctrine

### RADAR — **GAP CLOSED with a peer-reviewed source**

The provisional placeholder ("Tufte 1983 / Munzner 2014 proportional-encoding
doctrine — PROVISIONAL") is **replaced**. Neither work addresses radar charts on
these grounds.

**New citation:** Duan R, Tong J, Sutton AJ, Asch DA, Chu H, Schmid CH, Chen Y.
*"Origami plot: a novel multivariate data visualization tool that improves radar
chart."* **J Clin Epidemiol 2023;156:85–94.** DOI 10.1016/j.jclinepi.2023.02.020,
PMID 36822444, free full text PMC10599795.

Supports **both** shipped criticisms, verbatim from its Introduction:

> "First, the areas of the connected regions depend on the order of the axes on
> the plot."

> "Second, radar charts can distort the interpretation of data, as the area of the
> connected region is not proportional to the linear variable measures of interest
> but generally to their squares."

**Corroboration (secondary, not load-bearing):** Graham Odds, "A Critique of Radar
Charts", Scott Logic blog, 2011-09-23 — states both points in near-identical terms.

**Explicitly NOT usable:** Stephen Few's "Keep Radar Graphs Below the Radar" (2005)
is an authentic Few critique, but on **entirely different grounds** (circular
layout slows comparison; no reading direction; symmetry bias). He never mentions
area-squaring or axis-order. **Do not cite Few for the radar reason string.**

**Honest scope note:** both criticisms are *geometric/analytic* arguments in a
methods paper. No controlled perceptual user study measuring radar misjudgement
was found. The claims are published and peer-reviewed, not folklore — but they are
not psychophysics.

**Status: SIGNED — replace the placeholder in `dsx/checks/viz.py`.**
